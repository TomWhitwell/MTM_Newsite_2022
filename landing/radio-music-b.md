    <div class="lp-section-copy">
      <h2>The card is not admin. The card is composition.</h2>
      <p>It sounds like the boring part: copy files to a microSD card. But choosing what goes on the card is where the instrument starts. A bank can be a mood, a place, a song, a research folder, a mistake you want to keep making.</p>
      <ul class="lp-checks">
        <li>Up to 16 banks on the card</li>
        <li>Up to 250 files per bank</li>
        <li>Sequential and random subfolders</li>
        <li>Different settings per bank if you want to get nerdy</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
  </section>

    <div class="lp-control-map__copy">
      <p class="lp-kicker">The control story</p>
      <h2>There are only a few controls, so each one has to matter.</h2>
      <div class="lp-annotation"><strong>Station</strong><span>Which recording are we in? Or, with CV, which recording is the patch drifting towards?</span></div>
      <div class="lp-annotation"><strong>Start</strong><span>Where does the next reset begin? This is where long recordings become playable.</span></div>
      <div class="lp-annotation"><strong>Reset</strong><span>The button is not just "start again". It is the moment the patch asks the recording to speak.</span></div>
      <div class="lp-annotation"><strong>Pitch</strong><span>Tap Start and the same knob becomes speed. This is the bit that gets tape-ish, backwards, melodic or daft.</span></div>
      <div class="lp-annotation"><strong>Remote</strong><span>If you want all this under faders, plug in the Radio Remote. Very much optional. Very much fun.</span></div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section id="perform" class="lp-section lp-section--dark-grid">
    <div>
      <p class="lp-kicker">Radio Remote</p>
      <h2>The Remote is for people who change their minds while the music is playing.</h2>
      <p>Radio Music has settings on the SD card because that is powerful and cheap and reliable. Radio Remote exists because sometimes you do not want powerful and cheap and reliable. You want eight faders and a button that does the thing now.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="lp-remote-inline">
      <img src="/images/landing-prototypes/radio-remote-panel.png" alt="Radio Remote panel">
    </div>
    <div class="lp-card-grid">
      <div class="lp-card"><h3>Move the window</h3><p>Start and End faders let a long recording become a loop, a phrase, or a backwards little corner.</p></div>
      <div class="lp-card"><h3>Abuse speed</h3><p>Fine pitch, octave ranges, tape stops, freeze and the pleasingly grubby 90s mode.</p></div>
      <div class="lp-card"><h3>Change behaviour</h3><p>Loop off, normal loop, ping-pong, tuner modes and pulse divisions without editing a text file.</p></div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section id="generate" class="lp-section">
    <p class="lp-kicker">Patch examples</p>
    <h2>Features are easier to believe when they are attached to actual ideas.</h2>
    <div class="lp-lab-grid">
      <div><strong>Weather bed</strong><span>Put long outdoor recordings in a bank and let slow CV drift between them under a sequence.</span></div>
      <div><strong>Bad drum machine</strong><span>Put kicks, snares and little accidents in folders, then let reset pulses choose them.</span></div>
      <div><strong>Playable tape loop</strong><span>Use the Remote to close in on one phrase, reverse it, slow it down, then lose it again.</span></div>
      <div><strong>Sample clock divider</strong><span>Use the reset jack as a pulse output so the recording can tell the rest of the system where it is.</span></div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section id="learn" class="lp-band lp-band--dark">
    <div class="lp-section-copy">
      <p class="lp-kicker">Learning without a lecture</p>
      <h2>Sampling makes more sense when voltage can poke it.</h2>
      <p>Radio Music is a good first sampler because it is not trying to be everything. You can hear what start point means. You can hear what reset means. You can hear why backwards playback is both useful and silly. That is a better lesson than a screen full of tiny waveforms.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="lp-placeholder lp-placeholder--video">
      <strong>Video placeholder: blank card to first useful patch</strong>
      <span>Copy a few files, name one bank, plug the card in, patch reset from a clock, then turn Station by hand. This should look like "I can do that tonight", not like a tutorial series.</span>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section id="compare" class="lp-section">
    <p class="lp-kicker">How to choose</p>
    <h2>This is not the sampler for everyone. That is useful to say.</h2>
    <div class="lp-comparison">
      <div><strong>Radio Music alone</strong><span>Best if you want the small, direct, 4HP thing: Station, Start, Reset, pitch and CV.</span></div>
      <div><strong>Radio Music plus Remote</strong><span>Best if you want to perform speed, loop points, tuner modes and pulse behaviour with your hands.</span></div>
      <div><strong>A screen sampler</strong><span>Best if you need slicing, projects, waveform editing and exact arrangement. Radio Music is for immediacy, surprise and patch behaviour.</span></div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="lp-section lp-section--faq">
    <p class="lp-kicker">Trust, not hype</p>
    <h2>Answer the things people worry about.</h2>
    <details open><summary>What changed from the old Radio Music?</summary><p>Better sound, lower latency, modern file support, bigger cards, new playback modes, calibrated pitch input, card reader mode and Radio Remote support.</p></details>
    <details><summary>Will my old Radio Music files work?</summary><p>Yes, the old RAW files still work. WAV and AIFF work too, which is much nicer in 2026.</p></details>
    <details><summary>Is the SD card workflow annoying?</summary><p>It can be, if you try to make it perfect. Do not make it perfect. Put ten interesting files on a card and start there.</p></details>
