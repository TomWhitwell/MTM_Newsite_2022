      <p class="lp-kicker">Pocket-sized MIDI controller DIY kit</p>
      <h1>8mu is a tiny fader controller that does one sensible thing and one slightly daft thing.</h1>
      <p class="lp-lede">The sensible thing: eight proper faders sending MIDI. The slightly daft thing: an accelerometer inside, so lifting, tilting and turning the whole object can send MIDI too. It is smaller than a credit card, but not thinner, because faders have to be faders.</p>
      <div class="lp-actions">
        <a class="lp-button" href="#buy">Buy 8mu</a>
        <a class="lp-button lp-button--secondary" href="#uses">What would I use it for?</a>
      </div>
      <p class="lp-microcopy">USB-C MIDI, 3.5mm TRS MIDI, eight banks, web editor, four buttons, hidden LEDs, open-source firmware, and enough oddness to make it more than a generic little controller.</p>
    </div>

    <div><strong>Eight faders you can feel.</strong><span>Levels, macros, drawbars, sends, filter moves, little live-mix decisions.</span></div>
    <div><strong>Eight gestures you can map.</strong><span>Lift, tilt, rotate, turn over. Not always sensible. Often much more interesting than sensible.</span></div>
    <div><strong>Small enough to stay nearby.</strong><span>On a keyboard, in a laptop bag, stuck to a synth, next to a tiny modular, passed around a workshop.</span></div>

    <div class="lp-section-copy">
      <p class="lp-kicker">The actual promise</p>
      <h2>It gets a few important controls out from behind the glass.</h2>
      <p>Most electronic music software has too many parameters and not enough handles. 8mu is not trying to solve all of that. It gives you eight handles, plus a little bit of body movement, and that is often enough to change the way you play.</p>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="lp-section lp-section--story">
    <p class="lp-kicker">Why it lasts longer than one afternoon</p>
    <h2>It begins as "just a faderbank" and then becomes part of your habits.</h2>
    <div class="lp-story-grid">
      <div>
        <h3>Immediate</h3>
        <p>Plug it in. Open Ableton, Max, a synth, a browser thing, whatever listens to MIDI. Move a fader. Something changes. This is the whole point of MIDI, really.</p>
      </div>
      <div>
        <h3>Personal</h3>
        <p>Use the web editor to decide what the faders, buttons and gestures send. Turn off the noisy bits. Make one bank for one instrument and another for something completely different.</p>
      </div>
      <div>
        <h3>Open</h3>
        <p>If you never want to see code, you do not need to. If you do, it is an open little platform with Arduino and CircuitPython possibilities. Both things can be true.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

    <div class="lp-feature-run__list">
      <div><span>01</span><h3>Eight faders</h3><p>They are small, but they are still faders. That matters. A fader gives you a position, a memory, a thing to ride while listening.</p></div>
      <div><span>02</span><h3>Eight gesture channels</h3><p>Lift front, lift back, lift left, lift right, rotate clockwise, rotate anticlockwise, face-up and face-down. A small vocabulary for waving a MIDI controller around like you mean it.</p></div>
      <div><span>03</span><h3>Four buttons</h3><p>Use them as notes, controllers, bank navigation or little mode switches. Buttons are boring until they are in the right place.</p></div>
      <div><span>04</span><h3>LEDs behind the faders</h3><p>The LEDs glow through the slots when MIDI is moving and when banks change. A tiny bit of feedback without turning the panel into a Christmas tree.</p></div>
    </div>

  <section id="uses" class="lp-section lp-section--use">
    <p class="lp-kicker">What would someone like me do with it?</p>
    <h2>Here are six perfectly reasonable answers.</h2>
    <div class="lp-use-list">
      <div><strong>Ableton macros</strong><span>Eight faders for a rack, sends, levels, effect amounts, or the parameters you always forget to perform.</span></div>
      <div><strong>Small hardware setup</strong><span>TRS MIDI into a synth or box that needs a few more hands nearby.</span></div>
      <div><strong>Visuals</strong><span>TouchDesigner, Max, browser sketches, projection patches. MIDI is still a very useful old plumbing system.</span></div>
      <div><strong>Teaching MIDI</strong><span>One fader, one CC, one visible result. Much better than trying to explain channels with a diagram.</span></div>
      <div><strong>Gesture performance</strong><span>Tilt for filter, rotate for feedback, turn over to change a scene. Possibly ridiculous. Possibly exactly right.</span></div>
      <div><strong>Hacking</strong><span>A small finished object that can also become an unfinished object if that is your idea of fun.</span></div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="lp-section lp-section--faq">
    <p class="lp-kicker">Things people ask</p>
    <h2>The useful worries.</h2>
    <details open><summary>Do I need to code?</summary><p>No. The normal way to use 8mu is the web editor. The code is there because open things should stay open.</p></details>
    <details><summary>Will MIDI learn go mad because of the accelerometer?</summary><p>It can, which is why 8mu has MIDI Learn modes to temporarily turn off or tame the gesture channels while you map things.</p></details>
    <details><summary>Is it only for computers?</summary><p>No. USB is simplest, but the 3.5mm MIDI output means it can talk to hardware too, with Type A or Type B set in the editor.</p></details>
  </section>
