  <section id="why" class="lp-section">
    <p class="lp-kicker">The argument</p>
    <h2>The best controller is often the one that is already within reach.</h2>
    <div class="lp-comparison lp-comparison--four">
      <div><strong>Small enough to stay out</strong><span>If it has to be unpacked, plugged in and given desk space, it probably gets used less.</span></div>
      <div><strong>Useful enough to earn its place</strong><span>Eight faders cover a surprising amount of musical territory.</span></div>
      <div><strong>Odd enough to be memorable</strong><span>The accelerometer means it is not just a tiny copy of a bigger controller.</span></div>
      <div><strong>Open enough to keep changing</strong><span>Use the editor first. Hack the firmware later, or never. Both are allowed.</span></div>
    </div>
  </section>

      <p class="lp-kicker">First evening ideas</p>
      <h2>Do not sell "MIDI control". Sell the first useful thing someone will do.</h2>
      <div class="lp-annotation"><strong>Ableton rack</strong><span>Faders 1-8 become macro controls. Put the eight most performable decisions under one hand.</span></div>
      <div class="lp-annotation"><strong>Hardware synth</strong><span>TRS MIDI sends filter, resonance, envelope, wave shape or effects to a small setup with no computer in sight.</span></div>
      <div class="lp-annotation"><strong>Visual patch</strong><span>Faders move parameters. Tilt moves camera, blur, colour, feedback, something that looks nicely dangerous.</span></div>
      <div class="lp-annotation"><strong>Teaching setup</strong><span>Move one fader, see one value change. Then talk about channels, CCs and banks after people have heard the point.</span></div>
    </div>

  <section class="lp-band lp-band--paper">
    <div class="lp-section-copy">
      <p class="lp-kicker">The editor</p>
      <h2>Configuration should feel like labelling your toolbox, not programming a synth.</h2>
      <p>The browser editor is important because it makes the object less mysterious. You can see the faders move, choose what they send, turn off things you do not need, and save a bank before you ruin it in the traditional experimental fashion.</p>
      <ul class="lp-checks">
        <li>Choose CC numbers and MIDI channels</li>
        <li>Turn off gesture channels when they are not wanted</li>
        <li>Export and import the current bank</li>
        <li>Set Type A or Type B TRS MIDI</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
    <div class="lp-placeholder">
      <strong>Editor screenshot placeholder</strong>
      <span>Show the web editor connected to a real 8mu. The useful shot is a fader moving on the device and the matching value moving on screen. Also show channel zero, because turning things off is a genuinely important feature.</span>

  <section class="lp-section lp-section--dark-grid">
    <div>
      <p class="lp-kicker">The accelerometer</p>
      <h2>The gesture controls need jobs, otherwise they are just a party trick.</h2>
      <p>This page should be honest about that. Tilting a controller is not automatically musical. But if you give it the right job, it is lovely: a send, a filter, a visual change, something you want to happen because your hand moved.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="lp-card-grid">
      <div class="lp-card"><h3>Lift front</h3><p>Open a filter while the faders stay on levels.</p></div>
      <div class="lp-card"><h3>Rotate</h3><p>Push delay feedback or reverb send in a way that looks and feels intentional.</p></div>
      <div class="lp-card"><h3>Turn over</h3><p>Mute something, switch scene, change bank, make a visual patch blink. Probably do not map this to master volume.</p></div>
      <div class="lp-card"><h3>MIDI Learn mode</h3><p>Temporarily tame the gesture channels while you map things, because otherwise they quite reasonably shout over the faders.</p></div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section id="compare" class="lp-section">
    <p class="lp-kicker">What it is not</p>
    <h2>A useful product page tells people when not to buy the thing.</h2>
    <div class="lp-comparison">
      <div><strong>Not a big faderbank</strong><span>If you need sixteen long-throw faders for a mix, buy that. 8mu is for eight important controls close to the instrument.</span></div>
      <div><strong>Not a generic mini controller</strong><span>The accelerometer, banks, LEDs, editor and DIY story make it a specific Music Thing object, not a tiny black plastic rectangle.</span></div>
      <div><strong>Not a magic solution</strong><span>You still have to decide what to map. The nice bit is that once you do, the decision becomes physical.</span></div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="lp-section lp-section--faq">
    <p class="lp-kicker">Risk reversal</p>
    <h2>Finishable build. Reversible setup. No grand drama.</h2>
    <details open><summary>How hard is the build?</summary><p>The board is populated and programmed. You solder the faders and assemble the panels. Read the guide, line things up before soldering, and do not rush the big fader pins.</p></details>
    <details><summary>What if I make a terrible mapping?</summary><p>Good. Make several. Use the editor to change settings, export a bank you like, import one back, or reset the device if you have really painted yourself into a corner.</p></details>
    <details><summary>Can I mount it?</summary><p>Yes. Rubber feet, gel pads, Velcro, whatever works for the thing you are sticking it to. It was designed to be held, but it is happy to sit still.</p></details>
